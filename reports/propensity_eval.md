# Surrender propensity model: evaluation on synthetic data

Generated by `python -m ml.train_propensity`. All data is synthetic (see `data/synthetic_data.py`).
The labels come from a hand-written rule plus noise, so these numbers show how well the model
recovers that rule. They say nothing about how it would perform on real policies.

- Model: `gradient_boosting-seed42-n20000` (chosen on validation ROC AUC: logistic_regression 0.6975, gradient_boosting 0.7273)
- Data: 20,000 synthetic customers, 34,298 active policies, snapshot date 2026-01-01, seed 42
- Split by customer: 20,673 train / 6,853 validation / 6,772 test policies
- Surrender rate in the test split: 26.0%

## Test split

| Scores from | ROC AUC | PR AUC | Brier | Log loss | Lift in top 10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| logistic_regression | 0.704 | 0.435 | 0.175 | 0.526 | 2.01x |
| gradient_boosting | 0.732 | 0.472 | 0.168 | 0.506 | 2.09x |
| generator_probability (ceiling) | 0.746 | 0.494 | 0.164 | 0.496 | 2.26x |

The ceiling row scores the test split with the generator's own probabilities (the rule before the
coin flip). No model can beat it on average, so the gap to it is the useful number.

## Risk tiers on the test split (gradient_boosting)

| Tier | Policies | Share | Mean predicted | Observed surrender rate | Share of all surrenders |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| high | 345 | 5.1% | 55.0% | 62.9% | 12.3% |
| medium | 3,420 | 50.5% | 33.8% | 35.1% | 68.1% |
| low | 3,007 | 44.4% | 13.0% | 11.4% | 19.5% |

Tiers use the API's thresholds (`ml/decision.py`): high >= 0.5 (more likely than not to surrender),
medium >= 0.25 (above the average surrender rate). High-risk scores are queued
for human review before any retention outreach.
